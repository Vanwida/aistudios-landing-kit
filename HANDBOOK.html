Five minutes

Start a new project

  1. Copy this folder and rename the copy after the client — acme-landing.Never work inside the original kit. It stays clean for the next project.
  2. Open the Claude desktop app → Code → Open folder, choose your copy, and set the permission mode to Auto.
  3. Type /start and pick where you want to begin. Then just talk.Claude asks with clickable options — the client first, then the site's architecture. Give it a reference site and the options come pre-filled from it.
  4. Say design it. Claude drafts the design in Figma; you refine it in Figma, the way you always have.When you're happy, say import and Claude brings your refined design into the kit.
  5. Say next. Claude builds one section, and the board shows your Figma frame next to the screenshots at desktop, tablet and mobile.Say next to continue, or tell it what's off in your own words.
  6. When the page is complete: animate → review → ship.ship gives a preview link for the client; go live puts it on the real site.

That's the whole workflow. Two places to look: the preview at localhost:4321 and the board at localhost:4321/board — your project, rendered: the brief, every section with its frame beside the screenshots, the tokens as swatches, the review, the links, the save points. The board only exists on your computer; it is never published. Close the app any time; /help brings you back.

Reference

The commands

Two words to remember. The rest is talking.

/startWhere do you want to begin? New project · I have a finished Figma · I have a reference site · continue this project.
/helpWhere am I and what's next? Three lines of state, then the next actions as options — plus “something's wrong”.

Everything else is plain words: design it · import · next · animate · review · ship · go live · undo · this looks wrong. Shortcuts exist for each (/design, /figma, /build, /animate, /review, /ship, /undo, /fix) — you never need them.

The mental model

Ten ideas that explain everything

In teaching order. Each one maps to something you already do in Figma.

1

A website is a folder of files ≈ a Figma file

Just as a Figma file has pages and frames, a website is a folder: one file per section of the page, a file with the colours and type, a folder of images. When you open the folder in Claude Code, Claude edits those files. Nothing magical.

2

Localhost is your private preview ≈ prototype preview

http://localhost:4321 is the page running on your computer only. It refreshes itself every time Claude changes a file. It is not on the internet until you ship.

3

Tokens are variables ≈ Figma variables

Every colour, font size, spacing and radius lives in one file with names like --color-primary. Sections use the names, never raw values — change one value, the whole site updates. /figma fills this file from your design.

4

Sections are components ≈ components

One section of the page is one file. Claude builds them top to bottom and finishes each before the next. It's also why two designers can work on the same site without stepping on each other.

5

AI drafts, you refine — then fidelity

Claude draws the first Figma from the brief and the reference; you make it yours. From then on, Claude's job is to match your Figma, not improve it. Where Figma doesn't say — hover states, tablet layout, what moves — Claude picks the quiet option and tells you in one line. Your job is to look and say what's off, in your words: “more air above the title”, “the photo crop is wrong”, “too fast”.

6

Screenshots are how Claude sees

After every section Claude takes screenshots at 390, 834 and 1440 and compares them with your exported frame. A second critic looks with fresh eyes and lists the differences. You see both on the board. So always export your section frames as PNG into brief/figma/ — that's the reference everything is checked against.

7

Save points are version history ≈ version history

Every finished section, motion pass and review creates a save point. /undo lists them; you can jump back, and forward again. Nothing is ever lost.

8

GitHub is the Figma cloud, for code

GitHub keeps a copy of the project online with all its save points — like your Figma file living in the cloud. Every time Claude saves, that copy updates. The agency can see it; a teammate can pick it up; nothing depends on your laptop.

Repository
The project's online home. One per landing page.
Commit
A save point with a name.
Diff
What changed between two save points — like Figma's compare changes. You'll never read one; Claude does.
Branch
A parallel copy to work in, like a Figma branch. Claude works in draft; main is what's live. You never choose.
Merge
Applying a branch to main. Claude does it when you say go live. If two people changed the same section, Claude reconciles it and tells you in one sentence.
Pull request
A review request: “here's what changed, approve it to make it live.” Only if your agency wants a review gate.
9

Vercel is the publish button ≈ publish

Vercel takes the files from GitHub and puts them on the internet, with HTTPS and a URL, in about a minute. Every save gives a preview link to send the client; go live updates the real domain. The first time you log in with your browser; after that it's invisible.

10

Motion is a vocabulary, not a library

You don't need to know GSAP or Three.js. You need the words: reveal, stagger, parallax, pin, scrub, morph, ticker, marquee, curtain footer, magnetic hover. /inspire finds them in sites you love; /animate proposes a menu in those words; you say yes or no. The taste rules are built in — slow, few, intentional, and always a calm version for people who prefer less motion.

The only “prompting” you need

How to talk to Claude

  • Give it a brief, not a vibe. The /new-landing questions are the brief.
  • Show, don't describe. Paste a link, export a frame, point at a screenshot.
  • React like a creative director. “The hero feels cramped.” “This button is too loud.” “The story should hold longer.”
  • One thing at a time. Let it finish a section before asking for the next.
  • Say when you don't understand. It will re-explain without jargon.
  • Short answers are fine. “Yes.” “The second one.” “Black.”

What high quality looks like

Warm surfaces, one distinctive display face, generous space, media that carries the story, motion that moves at the pace of reading, and nothing that looks like a template.

If it looks like every AI page you've seen — purple gradients, three identical cards with icons in circles, things bouncing in from the sides — it isn't done. Say “this looks generic” and Claude knows what to change.

The calm list

When stuck

Nothing here is your fault, and nothing here loses your work.

The preview is blank or won't loadSay “blank page” — or /help → “something's wrong”. Claude checks the preview server and restarts it.
There's an error message in the chatDon't read it. Say “something's wrong”. If Claude asks what you were doing, answer in one line.
It doesn't look like my FigmaSay what's different in your words — “the heading is too small and breaks into three lines”. Or ask to see the screenshots next to your frame.
It changed something I didn't ask forSay “you changed X — put it back”. If it's more than one thing, say “undo” and pick the last good save point.
The motion feels wrongUse these words: too fast, too much, too bouncy, should hold longer, should be subtler. Or “remove the effect on the cards”.
It asked a technical question I don't understandSay “I don't know — choose the most standard option and tell me in one line”. That is always allowed.
It asked me to log in somewhereNormal the first time. A browser window opens; log in with your own account; come back and say “done”.
It's taking a long timeInstalling, building and screenshots take up to a minute. After three minutes with no message, say “status?”.
The app closed, or my laptop diedOpen the folder again and type /help. Everything is on the board and in save points.
The client wants changes after it's liveTell Claude what they said, in their words. Then “ship” for a preview link, “go live” when approved.
Nothing works and I'm out of patienceSay “I'm stuck — save everything and tell me where we are”. Then take a break. It'll be there.

The meta-rule

Describe what you see, in your words. Never diagnose.
